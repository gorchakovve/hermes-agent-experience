# P-025. Google AI reserve was not fully accepted

**Status:** partially confirmed: design work and isolated checks existed, but a full automatic reserve was not accepted into production in the cited records.

[Русская версия](../P-025.md)

## Symptom
After one Google AI egress path was restored, the system still had a single point of failure. Gemini/NotebookLM worked through one verified route, but there was no accepted reserve pool or automatic failover. Country labels, low ping, or anonymous HTTP 200 were explicitly rejected as sufficient acceptance.

## Conditions
The case belongs to the 2026-09-26 iMac failover design and related VPS Google AI reserve checks. At that point the working iMac route had one verified European node: a real Gemini response and NotebookLM list loading. Production changes were forbidden during the first design stage.

## Cause
The problem was architectural: sing-box `urltest` or a latency-only probe can pick a fast transport, but it does not prove that Google AI will avoid regional refusal or that signed-in Gemini/NotebookLM flows work. The design needed two separate concepts: manual node approval and current network health classification.

## Proposed fix
The plan chose a sing-box `selector` plus a small local health manager rather than urltest-only. Each candidate would have a separate loopback probe port, and selector changes would go through a loopback-only Clash API. The manager must not run background LLM calls or use cookies; it classifies transport/TLS, regional block, login/unknown, and latency.

## Review and verification
Codex confirmed that selector plus local checks was a suitable direction, but did not accept the project for production. It independently confirmed that the current iMac config matched the known working France config, no selector or failover LaunchAgent was installed, and rollback/test scenarios were only planned. Codex also noted that the claimed upper failover bound did not follow from the proposed timings and could be higher.

## Acceptance criteria
A node becomes approved only after transport/TLS, anonymous regional probing, and one manual signed-in check proving a real Gemini response and NotebookLM list loading. At least two approved nodes, failure fixtures, atomic config update, rollback, and independent Codex recheck are required. If all nodes are down, the system must keep the last working route and must not silently fall back to direct.

## Current public status
Do not claim `automatic reserve ready` for this incident. Verified: one working route and a safe design. A later record mentions Codex acceptance of a central reserve, but P-025 documents the earlier acceptance gap and the rule not to confuse a plan, anonymous endpoints, and signed-in proof.
