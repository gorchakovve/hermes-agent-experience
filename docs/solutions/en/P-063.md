# P-063. Server routing could not give devices their normal ISP for `.ru`

[Русский](../P-063.md) · [Index](../../problem-index.en.md)

**Status:** open; architectural limitation confirmed. An independent server-side policy was implemented, but the original “use the device’s ISP” requirement was not met.

## When and conditions
2026-09-26, centralized Google AI routing inside the AmneziaWG/VPS setup. The task was to extend routing to devices without breaking ordinary traffic or Russian sites.

## Symptoms
The user expectation was that all `.ru` traffic should leave through the ordinary ISP of the specific device. The actual server design only saw traffic after it had entered the Amnezia tunnel, so it could choose between server-side egress paths, not the phone or iMac’s physical ISP.

## Cause vs. hypothesis
This was not a single bad rule; it was a control-plane boundary. The server-side routing owner can send Google AI to an approved outbound and keep ordinary/RU traffic on the VPS, but it cannot return `.ru` traffic to the device’s local ISP without device-level split tunneling.

## What helped
Google AI policy and RU policy were separated. Google domains and required dependencies used a dedicated approved outbound, while ordinary and RU traffic stayed on the VPS. A blanket `.ru → RU exit` rule was not enabled because sampled RU sites worked through the VPS, and CAPTCHA also appeared on a tested RU egress.

## Verification
Synthetic clients from two live subnets showed Gemini HTTP 200, NotebookLM reaching Google login, ordinary Cloudflare traffic still using the German VPS, and Yandex/Mail.ru/Gosuslugi responding through the same general path. RU addresses were checked only as candidates, not accepted as a blanket policy.

## What is not proven
This does not prove all physical devices, IPv6-only apps, or specific Russian-IP-only services. If a service truly requires a Russian IP, it needs its own narrow policy and acceptance test.
